# Interview and user-test guide (about 20 minutes each)

Do these yourself; the report needs real people. Write notes the same day, in the person's own words where possible. Get a "yes" to use what they say, and use first names only or initials.

## A. Three people who tried a health app and stopped (Q1)

Find people like the customer in the report: adults who eat mostly home-cooked food. Ask, and mostly listen:

1. Which app, and what made you try it?
2. Walk me through the last time you used it. What did you actually do?
3. What was the most annoying thing? (Do not suggest answers.)
4. When did you stop, and what happened just before?
5. Did you ever feel bad or guilty about the app? When?
6. Did it understand what you eat? Give me one example.
7. Did money come into it?

For each: one line (who, app, how long, why they stopped) and one quote.

## B. User test of the live app (Q5), at least one person from the customer group

Give no instructions beyond: "Imagine you're trying to track your food. Use this link."

1. Watch the first two minutes without helping. Note where they hesitate or ask "what do I do?"
2. Ask them to log what they ate for lunch, in their own words, then another meal in a way they think might trip it up.
3. Ask: "What did you expect to happen when you tapped X?" "Did any number look wrong?"
4. Ask: "Would you open this tomorrow? Why or why not?"
5. Show the "Load a sample week" and ask if the budget note after a heavy day makes sense.

Record: who, what they did, what confused them, a bug or wrong estimate they found, and **what you changed** (link the commit). Honest negative findings score better than none.
